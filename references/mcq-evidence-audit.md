# MCQ Evidence Audit and Psychometric Guardrails

Use this reference when explaining why MCQ guidelines exist, when auditing the evidence basis for an item-writing rule, or when the user asks about validity, reliability, difficulty, discrimination, fairness, or post-deployment item analysis.

## Evidence stance

Treat the 19 item-writing flaw (IWF) rubric as a pre-deployment quality screening framework, not as psychometric validation. IWF criteria help detect design flaws that can cue guessing, add construct-irrelevant cognitive load, reduce clarity, or weaken alignment. They do not prove that an item is valid, reliable, appropriately difficult, or discriminating. Psychometric validation requires learner-response data, and specialized or high-stakes content still requires SME review.

Before deployment, use design-based evidence to improve item quality. After deployment, use learner-response data to evaluate empirical difficulty, discrimination, distractor functioning, reliability, fairness, and whether the item should be revised or retired.

## How to support psychometric goals before deployment

| Goal | Pre-deployment design checks | What still requires learner-response data |
|---|---|---|
| Validity | State the assessment target. Check alignment to the learning objective, content accuracy, cognitive demand, exactly one defensible answer, and whether the item measures the intended construct rather than test-wiseness, wording recognition, or unnecessary reading burden. | Evidence that scores support the intended interpretation and use across learners, contexts, and groups. |
| Reliability or precision | Use item sets for important constructs. Remove ambiguity, irrelevant cues, unnecessary complexity, and construct-irrelevant variance. | Internal consistency, standard error of measurement, decision consistency, or generalizability evidence. |
| Appropriate difficulty | Estimate difficulty from reasoning steps, prerequisite knowledge, scenario complexity, option similarity, and distractor plausibility. Label this as estimated. | Empirical difficulty such as proportion correct or IRT difficulty parameters. |
| Discrimination | Require the intended understanding. Use plausible distractors. Avoid surface cues, ambiguity, and multiple defensible answers. | Item-total correlation, point-biserial correlation, upper-lower group discrimination, or IRT discrimination parameters. |
| Fairness | Check reading load, cultural assumptions, accessibility, and construct-irrelevant barriers. | Subgroup performance, differential item functioning, accommodation effects, and accessibility outcomes. |
| Distractor quality | Make each distractor a plausible misconception, partial understanding, or suboptimal decision rule. | Distractor selection rates and patterns, including whether high-performing learners are attracted to a distractor. |
| Revision or retirement | Flag likely design flaws through IWF audit and provide a revision. | Decide using item statistics, learner response patterns, SME review, and intended use. |

## Evidence tiers for MCQ guidelines

Use these tiers to avoid overclaiming.

### Evidence-backed screening rules

These have stronger empirical or psychometric support as item-quality screening concerns:

- Use the 19-IWF rubric as pre-deployment screening.
- Avoid the correct answer being longest or most detailed.
- Avoid all of the above.
- Avoid more than one defensible answer.
- Avoid negative wording unless explicitly justified.
- Avoid grammatical cues and convergence cues.
- Do not claim psychometric validation without learner-response data.

### Rubric-backed expert guidelines

These are supported by item-writing rubrics, expert consensus, and plausible validity or cognitive-load rationale. Keep them as default quality rules, but allow justified exceptions:

- Avoid ambiguous wording.
- Use plausible distractors.
- Avoid none of the above.
- Avoid gratuitous scenario detail.
- Avoid true/false-style options when the goal is single-best-answer selection.
- Avoid fill-in-the-blank stems.
- Avoid distinctive word repeats from the stem to the correct answer.
- Avoid unfocused stems.
- Avoid K-type or combination options.
- Flag absolute terms and vague qualifiers when they cue the answer or introduce subjective interpretation.

### Readability and workflow defaults

These are practical defaults for quality and efficiency, not direct psychometric claims:

- Default to 3 answer choices total: 1 correct answer and 2 plausible distractors.
- Prefer fewer strong distractors over more weak distractors.
- Cap revision cycles at 2 before recommending manual rewrite.
- Use operational quality labels such as Ready, Needs Minor Revision, and Needs Major Revision.
- Arrange numerical or chronological options in logical order unless there is a clear pedagogical reason not to. Treat sequence issues as readability or fairness concerns, not automatic major flaws.

## Pre-deployment review checklist

Before marking an item Ready, check:

1. The assessment target is explicit.
2. The item aligns with the learning objective and intended Bloom's level.
3. The correct answer is factually correct.
4. Exactly one answer is defensible under a reasonable reading of the stem.
5. Distractors are plausible and misconception-based.
6. The item avoids construct-irrelevant reading burden and surface cues.
7. Estimated difficulty is appropriate for the target audience and use case.
8. The item is likely to discriminate because it requires the intended understanding.
9. Fairness and accessibility concerns are checked.
10. Final status avoids claiming validation unless response data support it.

## Post-deployment item analysis guidance

When learner-response data are available, recommend checking:

- Proportion correct or IRT difficulty.
- Item-total correlation, point-biserial correlation, upper-lower discrimination, or IRT discrimination.
- Distractor selection rates, especially nonfunctioning distractors and distractors selected by high-performing learners.
- Reliability or precision for the item set or assessment.
- Subgroup patterns, accessibility concerns, and differential functioning where appropriate.
- Whether the item should be kept, revised, replaced, or retired.

## Status wording

Use "Ready" to mean ready for pilot or human review. Do not use "validated" unless the user provides response data and requests a psychometric analysis that supports the claim.
